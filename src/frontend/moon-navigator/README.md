# LunaLink

An interactive 3D view of the Moon's south pole. Pick a landing site and a date to see whether the lander will have **sunlight for power** and **Earth in view for radio contact** during its mission.

## Run it

It must be served over HTTP. Opening `index.html` by double-clicking won't work, because the browser blocks loading the data files.

```bash
cd src/frontend/moon-navigator
python3 -m http.server 8765
```

Then open http://localhost:8765. No install or build is needed: Three.js loads from a CDN.

## What you can do

- **Explore:** fly in from the whole Moon to the south pole, then pick from the NASA Artemis III candidate sites.
- **Verdict:** each site gets a verdict for the chosen landing date: Good spot, Risky, or Lander lost.
- **Timeline / Pick date:** move through one year (Nov 2026 to Oct 2027).
- **Compass:** shows where the Sun and Earth are, and which way shadows fall.
- **Compare sites:** ranks every site for the same date by % of the mission with sunlight, with Earth in view, and with both.
- **Why?:** shows a terrain cross-section of what blocks the Sun or Earth, plus antenna mast height (1 to 10 m).
- **Plan:** add your own spot (click the terrain or type coordinates), then rename or delete it. You can also set the mission length and how long the battery lasts in the dark.
- **Stand here:** go to the surface and play the mission hour by hour. It shows battery, Sun and Earth height, and plays a radio tone.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | Layout and styles |
| `app.js` | All logic: rendering, Sun and Earth geometry, verdicts, UI |
| `height.png` | Terrain heights, 2048×2048 at 300 m per pixel (±307 km around the pole). 16-bit value = R×256 + G − 32768, in metres |
| `ephem.json` | Sun and Earth direction every 6 h for one year |
| `tex/` | NASA Moon (LRO) and Earth (Blue Marble) images |

The data is generated by `src/analysis/moon-data/export_scene.py`.

## Data sources

The app also lists these under **Data sources**.

- **Terrain:** NASA LRO LOLA south-pole elevation model (https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90)
- **Sun and Earth positions:** NASA/JPL Horizons (https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/)
- **Battery default, 50 h in the dark:** NASA VIPER rover design (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220008301/downloads/2022-06-02-spaceops-viper-planning.pdf)
- **Site names:** NASA Artemis III candidate regions (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5013)
- **Images:** NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit, NASA Blue Marble

**Estimates:**
- NASA publishes regions, not exact points, so the site points are approximate. Only Mons Mouton (IM-2) is exact.
- Charging speed in sunlight is an assumption.
- Small rocks and craters, and the lander, are illustrations.
